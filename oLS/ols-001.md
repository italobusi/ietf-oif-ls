CCAMP WG would like to inform OIF about the on-going work regarding the control and management of the pluggable optical transceiver modules.

This work can be split in 3 areas: 

1. Operator use cases and network scenarios needed for the control and management of the packet over optical networks where the optical transceivers are located inside pluggable modules hosted by packet network devices.

   These use cases include both coherent and non-coherent optical transceivers.

   See: [draft-ietf-ccamp-actn-poi-pluggable-usecases-gaps](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-ccamp-actn-poi-pluggable-usecases-gaps/)

1. YANG data models exposed by host devices or network controllers to provide optical module (pluggable and non pluggable) capabilities, configuration, states, and telemetry data.

   See:
      - [draft-ietf-ccamp-rfc9093-bis](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-ccamp-rfc9093-bis/)
      - [draft-ietf-ccamp-optical-impairment-topology-yang](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-ccamp-optical-impairment-topology-yang/)
      - [draft-ietf-ccamp-wdm-tunnel-yang](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-ccamp-wdm-tunnel-yang/)
      - [draft-ietf-ccamp-optical-path-computation-yang](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-ccamp-optical-path-computation-yang/)
      - [draft-ietf-ccamp-dwdm-if-param-yang](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-ccamp-dwdm-if-param-yang/)

1. Gap analysis of with respect to existing IETF work to identify if there are missing attributes defined in other SDOs/forum (including OIF), addressing the specific aspect of pluggability of transceiver modules.

   See: [draft-ietf-ccamp-actn-wdm-pluggable-modelling](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-ccamp-actn-wdm-pluggable-modelling/)

The CCAMP WG would appreciate any review from OIF experts on the content, parameter description, gaps and suggestion to improve these documents.

The CCAMP WG encourages the use of CCAMP WG mailing list (https://www.ietf.org/mailman/listinfo/ccamp/) as the most effective and expedient way of exchanging information, answering questions, and progressing any work.

Our understanding is that CMIS, as defined by OIF, and the YANG data models under definition in CCAMP WG are complementary since the CMIS is targeting the interface between the host and the pluggable modules while the YANG data models are targeting the northbound interfaces exposed by hosts or by network controllers. Therefore, we believe that not all the CMIS parameters need to be exposed by the host via YANG, but some of them (e.g., the carrier central frequency) may need to be exposed.


CCAMP WG is pretty confident that our YANG models are already aligned with 5.3 version of CMIS and would like to work on the new features supported by version 5.4. We’d like to have an open and fruitful relationship with OIF to be aligned with technical progress for CMIS context.
